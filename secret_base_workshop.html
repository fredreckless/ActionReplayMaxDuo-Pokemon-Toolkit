Record Workshop Make what a Pokémon Ruby, Sapphire or Emerald player hands over in the Record Corner: a Secret Base (the exact 160 bytes the game keeps, as an .sb3), the Mauville old man, Dewford trends, or a whole record, ready for the toolkit's mix command.

The room

How to use the workshop
  1. Choose a spot. “Kind of spot” and “Where” pick one of the 75 places a base can be. Changing spot starts that room afresh (you're asked whether to save first).
  2. Or press Generate. It furnishes the room for you: a theme, matching furniture, dolls on the desks and mats, and a clear path from the door to the owner and the PC. Press it again for another idea; Undo brings back what you had.
  3. To place something yourself: choose a kind below the room (Desks, Chairs…), then a decoration. Now click a tile in the room: that tile becomes the decoration's bottom-left corner. The dashed outline shows where it would go (red means it can't). Each pick places one; choose it again for another.
  4. To move or remove one: click it in the room (or in “In this base”). It gets a blue outline, and the arrow buttons under the room move it, a tile at a time. The keyboard works too: arrow keys move, Delete removes, Esc lets go.
  5. Where things can go (the game's own rules): desks, chairs, plants and ornaments stand on bare floor; posters hang on the back wall (the shaded top band); dolls and cushions sit on a desk's top or on a big mat. Up to 16 decorations; positions count 0 to 15 from the top-left.
  6. Then give the base an owner and at least one Pokémon on the right, read the Checks, and press Save the .sb3.

Hover over a tile to see what it is. “Use my ROM's graphics…” draws the room with the game's own pictures, read from your ROM in this browser only.

Pick a decoration below, then a tile in the room to place it. Its bottom-left corner goes on the tile you pick.

      The old man in Mauville's Pokémon Center
      
          
      How this works
            
        
              
      1. In Ruby and Sapphire one old man sits in Mauville City's Pokémon Center. He is one of five characters, and Record Mixing swaps him: after mixing with the DUO, your game meets the DUO's old man, whole, and he talks afresh.
        2. 
              
      2. Choose who he is and fill in what he says. The words are the game's Easy Chat words: type to search them. “Use my ROM's graphics…” at the top also reads the words from your ROM; without it, words are written as group:number.
        3. 
              
      3. Save the .om3, then mix with python duo_events.py mix --old-man FILE.om3 (add your bases with --base as usual), or put it in a whole record.

        Dewford trends
        
            
        
          
        How this works
            
          
              
        1. Dewford Town's people all talk about one two-word phrase: the trendiest one. A record carries up to five, each with how trendy it is now (up to its own maximum).
          2. 
              
        2. When records mix, the games pool everyone's phrases, keep the trendier copy of any phrase they share, and keep the five trendiest. Give yours a high trendiness to put it on everyone's lips.
          3. 
              
        3. Save them (one .dt3 each), then mix with python duo_events.py mix --trend FILE.dt3 …, or put them in a whole record.
          4. 
            
        
          
        
        
        
      
        
      
        
        
        
          
        The order the game puts them in
        
          
          
          
          Ruby's own order (trendiness, then its maximum, then the rest): the first is what Dewford talks about.

            TV shows
            
            
            
          
            How this works
            
              
              
            1. Every game makes TV shows about what its player does: interviews, captures, shopping, fishing, outbreaks. Record Mixing passes on the shows a player's game has already aired, and the receiving game puts them on its own TV.
              2. 
              
            2. Choose a kind and fill it in. Ruby and Sapphire know twelve kinds. A game won't take back a show its own player made, and keeps a show on the air only if its Pokédex has seen the Pokémon in it.
              3. 
              
            3. Save each show (.tv3), then mix with python duo_events.py mix --tv FILE.tv3 …, or put them all in a whole record.
              4.

                  Day-care letters
                  
          
                  How this works
            
                    
              
                  1. A Pokémon left at the day care holding Mail keeps its letter beside it. When records mix, letters are swapped between day-care Pokémon that hold nothing, and when the player collects their Pokémon, it's holding the swapped letter.
                    2. 
              
                  2. Write up to two letters, and say how many Pokémon are in "our" day care and which hold nothing (a letter can only go to and from those).
                    3. 
              
                  3. Save them (.dm3), then mix with python duo_events.py mix --mail FILE.dm3, or put them in a whole record.

                    A Battle Tower trainer
                    
            
                    
          
                    How this works
            
                      
              
                    1. Each game remembers its player's last Battle Tower run: their name, class, greeting, win streak and three Pokémon. Mixing adds the other player as a trainer in your Battle Tower, and you meet them when your win streak matches theirs.
                      2. 
              
                    2. Fill in the trainer and their three Pokémon (level 50 or 100, as the record's level). “Use the base's team” copies the first three from the Secret Bases tab. The checksum is worked out for you.
                      3. 
              
                    3. Save it (.bt3), then mix with python duo_events.py mix --tower FILE.bt3, or put it in a whole record.

                      A whole record
                      
      
                      How this works
        
                        
          
                      1. A record is everything one player hands over in the Record Corner: 20 Secret Bases, TV shows, news, the Mauville old man, Dewford trends, day-care letters, a Battle Tower trainer and a gift item (docs/RECORD_FORMATS.md). This tab puts one together as an .rm3 (Ruby and Sapphire's layout, which every game mixes with).
                        2. 
          
                      2. Start from a Ruby or Sapphire save backup (the record that game would send), an .rm3, or nothing; then press Put it in the record on any tab (a base, the old man, trends, TV shows and the rest) to add what's there, or add part files you already have.
                        3. 
          
                      3. A part left empty is handed back from the game's own record, so that part of the game doesn't change.
